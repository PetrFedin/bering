# Engineering, safety, environment, compliance

## Engineering P0
- GIS corridors with documented geodetic reference and georeferenced alternatives, land ownership and interfaces.
- Offshore bathymetry **not** confused with depth of excavated tunnel axis; boreholes, seismic and faults along actual alignment.
- Permafrost/ice-rich ground and temperature risks by physical segment, taliks and groundwater heat transfer, coastal erosion, flood and storm risks.
- TBM/rock and lining alternatives, settlement, grout, watertightness, portals, marine emergency response, RAMS, fire/smoke and evacuation, ventilation, rescue tunnels, traction power.
- New rail approaches on **both sides**, connection to US/Canadian network, train gauge/transshipment, clearance, train length and capacity, border controls, customs.

## Law and permissions P0
- Russia / USA / Canada rail and subsea permits and export control; sanctions and country-of-origin treatment for China → Russia → USA routes.
- Rights and consultation for Indigenous communities and local land/fisheries users; separate jurisdiction-specific obligations.
- Cross-border agreements/treaties, safety authority, operating entity, insurance and banking, personnel visas, cyber and power reliability.
- Competitive shipping and updated maritime environment/cost regime.

## Source comparators
European rail tunnel safety framework, Eurotunnel and Øresund commercial structures, Fehmarnbelt phased opening provide **analogues** only. Their metrics cannot simply be normalized by km and transplanted into Bering.

## Do not advance to construction budget
Until independent geotechnical work, current legal opinions, actual shipper commitments, full network design and an auditable BOQ / LCC exist, the intercontinental crossing remains **HOLD**.
